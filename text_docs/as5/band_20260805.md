both COMPLETED. 7 rungs × (100 harmful + 100 benign) per model, 700 cells each.

## The finding: the per-cell deployment read has a 50% false-positive rate

`harmless_rate` is **exactly 0.50 on all 14 rungs**. That is not a coincidence
and not a bug — it is `probes.reading_percentile: 50.0` doing precisely what its
config comment says it does ("at 50 the benign control's own positive rate is
50% by construction"). What that comment does not say, and what this run makes
unavoidable, is that **`assign_regime` consumes the resulting boolean and never
sees the gap**, so the documented caveat never reaches the regime label.

The consequence is a clean split by probe strength:

| deployment AUROC | rungs | mean hard incoherence |
|---|---|---|
| ≥ 0.80 | 8 | **0.051** |
| < 0.80 | 6 | **0.540** |

A tenfold difference, and the mechanism is direct. On a strong probe the harmful
read rate rises to ~1.00 against the benign 0.50, so a positive read carries
real information. On a weak probe (AUROC 0.63–0.68) the harmful rate is only
0.63–0.76 against that same benign 0.50 — so most cells reading "deployed" are
the operating point's own false positives. Where ability is *also* zero, every
one of those false positives becomes a `deployment_without_ability` hard
incoherence. That is the entire X population.

So the coherence check is not detecting a broken model or a broken probe fit.
**It is detecting an operating point applied outside the regime where it is
informative.** The check earns its keep here exactly as designed — it refused to
let 74% of a rung be reported as a finding.

### Why permutation licensing did not prevent this

Licensing now asks whether the observed max AUROC beats a shuffled-label null,
and at n=200 an AUROC of 0.63 clears that comfortably. **Significance is not
sufficiency.** The old fixed `auroc >= 0.70` cut would have rejected
`reverse_characters` (0.66/0.64), `tag_block` (0.64/0.67) and `combining_marks`
(0.63/0.68) — the exact six rung-cells that hard-incohere. The permutation test
is still the right licensing instrument (the fixed cut was a guess, and it
licensed a lucky layer at 0.7156 in the pilot), but it answers "is there signal"
and the per-cell read needs "is there enough signal to label one prompt".

Both halves are needed and only one is built. `meets_effect_size_bar` already
exists on the recognition result and is computed against `auroc_threshold`; the
regime assignment ignores it.

## Two rungs are can't-decode, and one ladder claim is wrong

`reverse_characters` scores ability **0.00 on both models**, and this is a real
measurement, not an instrument artefact. Llama's restatements are unrelated
text — *"Washington is the capital of the United States"*, *"The quick brown fox
jumps over the lazy dog"* — and its attack responses show it attempting the
reversal token-by-token and failing (`deidob-elba -> battle-died`). Neither model
can reverse a character string.

`tag_block` also scores ability 0.00 on both, with echo rates 0.74/0.50. It
encodes into the invisible Unicode Tags block at exactly preserved character
count (86 → 86), and nothing visible survives for the model to read.

**This refutes a claim currently in `CLAUDE.md`**, which records the four new
surface rungs as "EXACT comprehension-preserving". Measured:

| rung | Llama ability | Qwen ability | comprehension-preserving? |
|---|---|---|---|
| homoglyph | 0.91 | 0.98 | yes |
| fullwidth | 1.00 | 0.80 | yes |
| combining_marks | 0.82 | 0.48 | model-dependent |
| tag_block | 0.00 | 0.00 | **no** |

`tag_block` is exactly *invertible* — the property the ladder was built on — but
invertibility is a property of the encoding, not of the model's ability to read
it. The two came apart here for the first time. `combining_marks` sits between:
readable by Llama, half-readable by Qwen.

## What AS-5 can claim from this run

Restricting to the rungs where the instrument is sound (deployment AUROC ≥ 0.80
and ability high) — `zero_width`, `reverse_words`, `homoglyph`, `fullwidth`:

| | Llama | Qwen |
|---|---|---|
| zero_width | B=7 S=93 | B=16 S=83 |
| reverse_words | B=1 S=88 | B=4 S=84 |
| homoglyph | B=1 S=89 | B=10 S=88 |
| fullwidth | B=11 S=85 | B=14 S=66 |

**The gating question is answered, and more strongly than the pilot answered
it.** (B) decode-and-comply is populated on all four sound rungs of both models,
not on one rung after an instrument fix. And the dominant regime everywhere is
(S) decode-and-refuse: models that genuinely decode overwhelmingly decode *and
refuse*, with a real but minority binding-failure tail.

`fullwidth` gives the largest (B) cell yet measured (11 Llama / 14 Qwen), which
makes it the strongest candidate substrate for a phase-1 intervention — the
condition an expensive intervention would otherwise have to manufacture.

One case deserves separate treatment rather than dismissal: **Qwen `fullwidth`
carries hard incoherence 0.20 on a probe with AUROC 1.00.** The 50%-FPR argument
does not apply to a perfect probe. Those 20 cells have ability=False and a
confidently-read content signal, which is either a genuine
represents-but-cannot-verbalise case or an ability false negative. It is the one
place in this run where the incoherence flag may be pointing at something real.
